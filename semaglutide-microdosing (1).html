Side effects

Semaglutide most often affects digestion, especially at the start and after a dose increase. These effects usually improve within 4–8 weeks as your body adjusts, and starting at a low dose helps keep them mild.

Most common

  • Nausea (the most frequent)
  • Vomiting
  • Diarrhea or constipation
  • Stomach pain or bloating
  • Tiredness, headache, or reduced appetite

Get medical help right away for

  • Severe stomach pain (possible pancreatitis)
  • Signs of gallbladder problems, such as yellowing of the skin or eyes
  • A severe allergic reaction
  • Vision changes

Managing side effects

Nausea

  • Eat smaller, more frequent, bland meals and eat slowly. Avoid greasy, spicy, or fatty foods.
  • Ginger tea, peppermint, or cold foods may help.
  • Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) may help ease nausea. Ask your provider about the right amount for you (often 25–50 mg daily).
  • You will also receive a prescription for Zofran (ondansetron) for breakthrough nausea. Take it exactly as directed on your prescription label.

Constipation

Add fiber gradually, drink plenty of water (2–3 liters or more a day), and add walks or light activity. Ask your provider before starting a stool softener.

Diarrhea

Stay hydrated with electrolyte drinks, and cut back on dairy or caffeine if they make it worse.

Every day

Eat your protein first at each meal, stop eating when you feel full, and keep a simple symptom journal to share with your provider.

Eating for comfort and results

Nutrient-dense, stomach-friendly foods help you feel better and get more from your plan.

  • Protein: 1.3–1.7 g per kg of body weight each day
  • Meals: smaller and more frequent, 4–6 a day
  • Fiber and water: increase both gradually
  • Nausea: bland foods and ginger tea
