Tirzepatide

Enchanted
MEDICAL AESTHETICS

Tirzepatide is a once-weekly injection used for weight management and blood sugar control. It works on two natural gut hormones, GLP-1 and GIP, which is why it is called a dual agonist. Always follow the instructions from your Enchanted provider. This sheet does not replace medical advice.

How Tirzepatide works

GLP-1

Tirzepatide mimics GLP-1, a hormone your gut releases after eating. It helps the pancreas release insulin when blood sugar is high, lowers glucagon (a hormone that raises blood sugar), slows stomach emptying so you feel full longer, and quiets hunger signals in the brain.

GIP

Tirzepatide also mimics GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide). GIP boosts insulin release only when blood sugar is elevated, supports fat metabolism, and may improve insulin sensitivity. Together, GLP-1 and GIP have a stronger effect on weight and blood sugar than GLP-1 alone.

What results to expect

Results vary with diet, activity, starting weight, consistency, and other factors. In the SURMOUNT clinical trials, adults without type 2 diabetes who used brand-name Tirzepatide with lifestyle changes for 72 weeks (about 1½ years) lost an average of 15–22% of their body weight, at doses up to 15 mg weekly. At 5 mg weekly, average loss was about 15%. Many people lost 10–20%, and some lost more than 25%.

These results come from brand-name Tirzepatide over 72 weeks. Your own results will depend on your dose, your time on treatment, and the habits you build alongside it.

How to give your injection

See your Enchanted Subcutaneous Injections guide for step-by-step pictures and tips. The short version:

  1. Wash your hands well.
  2. Gather your vial, an insulin syringe, alcohol pads, and a sharps container.
  3. Check the vial. The liquid should be clear and colorless to slightly yellow. Don’t use it if it is cloudy, discolored, or has particles.
  4. If refrigerated, you may let the vial reach room temperature for comfort.
  5. Wipe the vial top with an alcohol pad.
  6. Pull air into the syringe equal to your dose (for example, 12.5 units of air for a 12.5-unit dose).
  7. Insert the needle into the vial and push the air in.
  8. Turn the vial upside down and draw your exact dose. Tap out any air bubbles.
  9. Choose a site: abdomen (at least 2 inches from the belly button), thigh, or upper arm. Rotate sites each week.
  10. Clean the site with an alcohol pad. Gently pinch the skin, insert the needle at 90° (or 45° if you are lean), and inject slowly.
  11. Remove the needle and place the syringe straight into your sharps container. Never recap.
  12. Never share syringes or vials. Use a new syringe every time.

Mild redness, swelling, or itching at the site? Apply a cold compress, and let your provider know if it is severe.

Side effects

Side effects are most common as your body adjusts and usually ease over time. Contact your provider if they are severe or don’t go away.

Common

  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Diarrhea or constipation
  • Stomach pain or discomfort
  • Indigestion or heartburn
  • Decreased appetite
  • Redness, swelling, or pain at the injection site

Get medical help right away for

  • Severe stomach pain, with or without chills, fever, or lightheadedness (possible pancreatitis)
  • Yellowing of the skin or eyes, or dark urine (possible gallbladder problems)
  • Blurred vision or other vision changes
  • Signs of low blood sugar if you take other diabetes medications: shakiness, sweating, confusion, fast heartbeat
  • Allergic reaction: rash, itching, swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing
  • Dehydration: dry mouth, dizziness, urinating less
  • Mood changes, including anxiety, depression, or thoughts of suicide
  • A lump in the neck or trouble swallowing

Managing side effects

Nausea

  • Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) may help ease nausea. Ask your provider about the right amount for you (often 25–50 mg daily).
  • A prescription for Zofran (ondansetron) will be sent to your pharmacy of choice in case you need extra relief. Take it as directed on the label.
  • Eat small, frequent meals, avoid fatty or spicy foods, and stay hydrated.

Constipation

Tirzepatide slows digestion, which can cause constipation. To stay regular:

  • Drink at least 8–10 glasses of water a day.
  • Eat high-fiber foods such as fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and beans, aiming for 25–30 g of fiber a day.
  • Move every day, such as a 30-minute walk.
  • Ask your provider before using a stool softener (docusate), fiber supplement (psyllium), or gentle laxative (MiraLAX).
  • Call your provider if you go 3 or more days without a bowel movement or have severe discomfort.

Who should not use Tirzepatide

Do not use Tirzepatide if you have:

  • A personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma
  • Multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2)
  • Severe gastrointestinal disease, such as gastroparesis
  • A known allergy to Tirzepatide or any of its ingredients

Tell your provider if you have:

  • A history of pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, or kidney problems
  • Diabetic retinopathy
  • A higher risk of dehydration
  • Depression or thoughts of suicide
  • Surgery or a procedure with anesthesia coming up

Medications. Tell your provider about every medication and supplement you take. Using Tirzepatide with insulin or other diabetes medications raises the risk of low blood sugar.

Birth control and pregnancy. Tirzepatide can make birth control pills less effective. If you take the pill, switch to a non-oral method or add a backup method for 4 weeks after starting and for 4 weeks after each dose increase. Do not use Tirzepatide if you are pregnant or planning a pregnancy.

Follow-up and labs

  • Follow-up visits: Week 8, Week 16, and Week 24 from your start date, or as your provider adjusts.
  • Labs every 6 months: CBC, CMP, thyroid panel (TSH, free T3, free T4), lipid panel, A1C, fasting insulin, and B12.

Storage

  • Store the vial in the refrigerator at 36–46°F (2–8°C). Do not freeze.
  • Keep it in its original packaging to protect it from light.
  • Once in use, it may be kept at room temperature (up to 86°F / 30°C) for up to 21 days. Don’t return it to the refrigerator after that.
  • Throw it away after 21 days at room temperature or after its Beyond Use Date.
  • Keep it away from children and pets. Ask your pharmacist how to dispose of unused medication.
