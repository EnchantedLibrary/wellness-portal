🌿 Bowel Regimen — Constipation Prevention
⚠ Constipation is one of the most common side effects of GLP-1 medications. Semaglutide and Tirzepatide slow gastric emptying and gut motility (peristalsis). This is expected — but it must be managed proactively from Day 1. Do not wait until you are constipated to start the regimen below.
What is constipation?

Constipation is when you are unable to pass a stool or have very hard stools. Most people have bowel movements every 1–3 days. If you are moving less frequently than usual and having hard stools, you are likely constipated. Contact us if you have gone more than 72 hours without a bowel movement.

🤢 Nausea Management
💊 Every patient in our program receives a Zofran (ondansetron) prescription. This is sent to your pharmacy of choice at program start. Fill it right away — have it on hand before your first injection so you are prepared.
Why nausea happens

GLP-1 medications slow stomach emptying — this is part of how they reduce appetite and promote weight loss. In the early weeks, your body is still adjusting to this change, which can cause nausea. For most patients it is mild and improves significantly within the first 4–8 weeks. Nausea is most common in the 24–48 hours after your injection and typically lessens as your dose stabilizes.

Your Zofran (Ondansetron) Prescription
How to use Zofran for nausea
  • Take 4 mg or 8 mg as prescribed — place under your tongue or swallow with water
  • Take at the first sign of nausea — do not wait until it becomes severe
  • May be taken up to every 6–8 hours as needed
  • Can be taken before your injection if you know nausea is likely
  • Safe to use with food or on an empty stomach
Additional tips to reduce nausea
🍽 Eating habits
  • Eat small, frequent meals — 4 to 5 small meals rather than 2 to 3 large ones
  • Eat slowly and stop when full — do not overeat
  • Avoid fatty, greasy, or fried foods
  • Avoid spicy foods until nausea settles
  • Avoid carbonated drinks
  • Do not lie down immediately after eating
💊 Supplements & remedies
  • Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine) 25–50 mg daily — discuss dose with your provider
  • Ginger tea or ginger chews
  • Peppermint tea
  • Small amounts of bland foods (crackers, toast, rice)
  • Stay upright for 30 minutes after eating
⚠ Contact us if: Nausea is severe or does not improve with Zofran · You are unable to keep fluids down for more than 24 hours · You have signs of dehydration (dry mouth, dizziness, dark urine, no urination) · Nausea is accompanied by severe stomach pain. Call 911 if symptoms are severe.
🏋️ Exercise — Why It's Non-Negotiable on GLP-1s
⚠ GLP-1 medications reduce your appetite significantly — which means you will eat less. When you eat less without exercising, your body loses both fat AND muscle. Losing muscle lowers your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), making it harder to keep the weight off long term. Exercise is what protects your metabolism while you lose weight.
What happens to your BMR when you lose weight

Your BMR is the number of calories your body burns at rest just to keep you alive — breathing, circulation, cell repair. When you lose weight, your BMR naturally decreases because there is less body mass to maintain. This is normal. The problem is when muscle is lost alongside fat — muscle is metabolically active tissue, meaning it burns calories even when you are not moving. The more muscle you lose, the lower your BMR drops, and the fewer calories you burn at rest.

This is why many people regain weight after stopping GLP-1 medications — their BMR has dropped and their metabolism is slower than before they started. Resistance exercise is the most effective way to preserve muscle and protect your BMR during weight loss.

Minimum weekly exercise recommendations
💪 Resistance / Strength Training
Minimum: 2× per week
  • Most important exercise type on GLP-1s
  • Preserves and builds lean muscle mass
  • Directly protects your BMR
  • Examples: weights, resistance bands, bodyweight (squats, push-ups, lunges)
  • 20–45 minutes per session is sufficient
🚶 Cardio / Aerobic Exercise
Minimum: 150 min/week moderate
  • Supports heart health and fat burning
  • Improves insulin sensitivity
  • Examples: brisk walking, cycling, swimming, elliptical
  • 30 minutes daily walking is a great starting point
  • Can be broken into 10-minute increments
🥗 Protein + Exercise — the combination that protects your muscle

Exercise alone is not enough — you must eat adequate protein for your body to build and maintain muscle. Aim for at least 1 gram of protein per pound of your goal body weight per day. Prioritize protein at every meal. On GLP-1 medications your appetite is reduced, so it takes intentional effort to hit your protein target. If you are not eating enough protein and not exercising, the weight you lose will come from both fat and muscle — setting you up for rebound.

If you are new to exercise
  • Start with 15–20 minute walks daily — build from there
  • Add 2 resistance sessions per week using light weights or bodyweight
  • Any movement is better than none — consistency matters more than intensity
  • GLP-1 medications improve energy over time — many patients find exercise gets easier as weight comes off
  • Listen to your body — nausea after eating can affect workout timing; wait 1–2 hours after meals
