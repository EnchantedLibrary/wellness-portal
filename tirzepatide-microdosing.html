4-Week Tirzepatide Microdosing Plan

MEDICAL AESTHETICS

This plan starts you on a very low dose of Tirzepatide and increases it gently, so your body can adjust with fewer side effects. Always follow the instructions from your Enchanted provider.

Feeling sick after a dose increase? Don’t increase again on your own. Call us. Your provider may keep you at your current dose longer or move you back to the last dose that felt good.

Injection tips. Inject once a week on the same day, under the skin of your abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. Rotate sites each week and use a new syringe every time. Keep the vial refrigerated, and let it reach room temperature before injecting. See your Enchanted Subcutaneous Injections guide for step-by-step instructions.

Missed a dose? Take it within 4 days of your usual day. If more than 4 days have passed, skip it and take your next dose on schedule. Never double up. If you miss 2 or more doses in a row, call us before restarting.

Side effects

Tirzepatide most often affects digestion, especially at the start and after a dose increase. These effects usually ease as your body adjusts, and starting at a very low dose helps keep them mild.

Most common

  • Nausea (the most frequent)
  • Vomiting
  • Diarrhea or constipation
  • Stomach pain, bloating, indigestion, or heartburn
  • Tiredness, headache, or reduced appetite
  • Redness, swelling, or itching at the injection site

Get medical help right away for

  • Severe or persistent stomach pain, with or without vomiting (possible pancreatitis)
  • Pain in the upper right belly, fever, or yellowing of the skin or eyes (possible gallbladder problems)
  • Signs of dehydration: dry mouth, dizziness, or urinating much less
  • A severe allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing
  • Vision changes, a lump in the neck, or trouble swallowing
  • Mood changes, including thoughts of suicide

Managing side effects

Nausea

  • Eat smaller, more frequent, bland meals and eat slowly. Avoid greasy, spicy, or fatty foods.
  • Ginger tea, peppermint, or cold foods may help.
  • Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) may help ease nausea. Ask your provider about the right amount for you (often 25–50 mg daily).
  • You will also receive a prescription for Zofran (ondansetron) for breakthrough nausea. Take it exactly as directed on your prescription label.

Constipation

Add fiber gradually, drink at least 64 oz of water a day, and walk or move every day. Ask your provider before starting a stool softener or MiraLAX, and call us if you go 3 or more days without a bowel movement.

Diarrhea

Stay hydrated with electrolyte drinks, and cut back on dairy or caffeine if they make it worse.

Every day

Eat your protein first at each meal, stop eating when you feel full, and keep a simple symptom journal to share with your provider.

Eating for comfort and results

  • Protein first: lean meat, fish, eggs, tofu, and Greek yogurt protect your muscle and keep you full.
  • Meals: small and frequent instead of large portions.
  • Carbs: cut back on sugary and processed foods.
  • Fiber and water: increase both gradually.
  • Limit: fried and greasy food, carbonated drinks, alcohol, and too much caffeine.
  • Pace: losing more than about 2 lbs a week can cost you muscle. Steady is the goal.

See the GLP-1 Friendly Foods guide for a full food list and sample day.

Safety reminders

  • Tell your provider about every medication you take. Tirzepatide with insulin or other diabetes medications raises the risk of low blood sugar.
  • Tirzepatide can make birth control pills less effective. If you take the pill, use a non-oral method or add a backup method for 4 weeks after starting and for 4 weeks after each dose increase, including Weeks 2, 3, and 4 of this plan. Do not use Tirzepatide if you are pregnant or planning a pregnancy.
  • Store the vial in the refrigerator. Once in use, it may be kept at room temperature (up to 86°F) for up to 21 days. Don’t return it to the refrigerator after that.
